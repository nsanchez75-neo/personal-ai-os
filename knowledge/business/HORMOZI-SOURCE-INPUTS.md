## High-value mechanisms

### 1. Market and niche before persuasion
Prefer a specific customer segment with a real, urgent and economically viable problem. Evaluate market quality before optimizing persuasion tactics.

### 2. Value engineering
Use the source's value equation as a diagnostic heuristic:

Perceived Value ≈ (Desired Outcome × Perceived Probability of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)

Operational levers: increase outcome relevance; increase credible probability through proof and delivery quality; reduce time-to-value; reduce customer effort and friction. This is a heuristic, not a law of economics.

### 3. Offer architecture
Reusable components: core outcome; bonuses for adjacent problems; real scarcity/capacity constraints; real urgency/deadlines; risk-reduction mechanisms; pricing and packaging aligned with value and delivery economics.

### 4. Money-model architecture
Candidate patterns: attraction offers; upsells; downsells; continuity; DIY/DWY/DFY delivery; offer ladders. Selection depends on customer need, unit economics, operational capacity, trust, compliance and strategic fit.

### 5. Sales system
CLOSER is a candidate conversation structure: Clarify, Label, Overview Pain, Sell the Vacation, Explain Away Concerns, Reinforce the Decision. Acquisition channels identified in the source: warm outreach, cold outreach, content and paid ads. Lead-getter categories: customers/referrals, employees, affiliates and agencies/partners.

### 6. Cash conversion and capital discipline
Track time from customer acquisition to cash sufficient to cover delivery and, where appropriate, future acquisition. Analyze alongside CAC, gross margin, contribution margin, payback, retention and working-capital risk. Do not assume credit-card-funded advertising is universally appropriate.

### 7. Leverage
Four broad categories: collaboration/people, capital, content and code. Use them as a lens for increasing output per unit of scarce founder time or capital.

### 8. Time-to-value
Design an early customer win where it is genuinely possible and useful. Track time-to-first-value separately from time-to-promised outcome.

### 9. Product quality and referrals
Treat product/service quality, customer outcomes and referral behavior as connected variables, but measure them rather than assuming causality.
